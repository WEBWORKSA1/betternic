Trademark Conflict Search

Check a domain for trademark conflicts: famous-brand screening plus one-click searches in USPTO, WIPO Global Brand Database, EUIPO, CIPO and UKIPO.

A domain that contains someone else's trademark can be taken from you through a UDRP complaint, and can expose you to legal claims. Before you buy, register or develop a name, check it. This tool screens the name against a list of famous brands and opens pre-filled searches in the main trademark databases so you can review live records yourself.

How to use the Trademark Conflict Search

  1. Enter the domain or brand term.
  2. Press Check to run the famous-brand screen.
  3. Open each trademark office search and review matching marks in relevant classes.
  4. Run the UDRP risk self-assessment if you find a close match.

How it works

The screen compares the name — and its segmented words — with well-known marks and flags exact and contained matches. Database links open the official search tools: USPTO trademark search, WIPO Global Brand Database, EUIPO eSearch, Canada's CIPO database, UK IPO and IP Australia. Trademark rights depend on goods and services, territory and use, so a match is a reason to investigate, not an automatic conflict.

When to use it

  • Pre-purchase due diligence
  • Startup naming
  • Avoiding UDRP exposure in a portfolio

Pro tips

  • Generic dictionary words can be trademarks in specific classes and still be fine to own as domains — the use matters.
  • Avoid typos of famous brands entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Can I own a domain that matches a trademark?

Often yes, if the term is generic and you don't use the domain to target the trademark owner's market. Using it in bad faith is what creates UDRP risk.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a research aid. For anything important, consult a trademark lawyer.

Which office should I search?

The offices covering the markets where the name will be used — at least USPTO and WIPO for global brands.
