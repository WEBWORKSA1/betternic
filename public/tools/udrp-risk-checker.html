UDRP Risk Self-Assessment

Answer ten questions to estimate your exposure under the UDRP's three elements: confusing similarity, rights or legitimate interests, and bad faith.

The Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy (UDRP) lets trademark owners recover domains registered and used in bad faith. A complainant must prove three elements. This self-assessment walks through each one with plain-language questions and shows where your risk is concentrated — useful before you buy a domain, and when you receive a complaint or a demand letter.

How to use the UDRP Risk Self-Assessment

  1. Answer each question honestly.
  2. Review the risk level for each of the three UDRP elements.
  3. Follow the suggested next steps; consult a lawyer for real disputes.

How it works

The questions map to UDRP Paragraph 4(a): (i) the domain is identical or confusingly similar to a trademark in which the complainant has rights; (ii) the registrant has no rights or legitimate interests in it; (iii) it was registered and is being used in bad faith. All three must be proven for a complaint to succeed. Paragraph 4(c) lists ways to show legitimate interests, and 4(b) lists examples of bad faith. The scoring is a teaching aid, not a prediction.

When to use it

  • Pre-purchase legal screening
  • Responding to a demand letter
  • Portfolio risk reviews

Pro tips

  • Keep evidence of why and when you registered each domain.
  • Never offer a domain for sale to a trademark owner for a price far above your costs in your first contact.
  • Panels can find reverse domain name hijacking against complainants who abuse the process.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a UDRP case take?

Usually around 45–60 days from filing to decision with WIPO or other providers.

What does a UDRP cost?

Provider fees for a single-panelist case with WIPO start around USD 1,500 for one to five domains; legal fees are extra.

Can I appeal a UDRP decision?

There is no appeal within UDRP, but either party can go to court; the registrar waits 10 business days before transferring to allow a court filing.
