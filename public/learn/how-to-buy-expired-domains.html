How to Buy Expired Domains: Auctions, Backorders and Due Diligence

Find, evaluate and buy expired domains: how expired auctions and backorders work, what to check before bidding, and how to avoid toxic names.

Every day tens of thousands of domains expire. A small share are genuinely valuable: names with history, links, type-in traffic or simply strong words that an owner forgot to renew. Buying expired domains well is about filtering ruthlessly and checking carefully.

Where expired domains go

After a domain expires, most registrars keep it for a grace period and offer it at an expired-domain auction. If nobody buys it, it moves to redemption (about 30 days), then pending delete (5 days), and finally drops, when drop-catching services race to register it. The domain lifecycle guide explains each stage, and the drop date calculator estimates the dates for any name.

Three ways to buy

  1. Expired auctions. Registrar auctions during the grace period. BetterNic's auction aggregator scores the day's inventory so you can start with a shortlist.
  2. Backorders. Pay a drop-catching service to try to register the name when it drops. If several people backorder the same name, it usually goes to a private auction.
  3. Hand registration after the drop. Names nobody wanted are simply available to register — useful for niche or long-tail names.

Filter first

Start with structure: length, extension, no hyphens, real words or pronounceable brandables. Then look at signals that suggest value: age, bids, traffic and keyword demand. The aggregator lets you filter by extension, length, price and keyword and sort by BetterNic score, bids or traffic.

Due diligence on finalists

  • History: open the website history. Was it a real business, a blog, or a spam network?
  • Reputation: run the reputation check for malware, spam and blocklist issues.
  • Trademarks: run the trademark check. Names that target a brand are a liability at any price.
  • Email: if the domain received email, expect stray messages and decide how you will handle them responsibly.

Set your maximum before you bid

Decide your maximum from a wholesale valuation and stick to it. Auctions reward discipline: many investors make their money on names that attracted few bids rather than on headline names with dozens of bidders.

After you win

Add the name to your portfolio tracker with the renewal date, decide whether to develop, park or sell, and list it with a clear price if you intend to resell.
