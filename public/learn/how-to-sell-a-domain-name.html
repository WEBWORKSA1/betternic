How to Sell a Domain Name: Pricing, Listing, Outreach and Safe Transfer

Step-by-step guide to selling a domain name: set a price, list it, generate inquiries, negotiate, use escrow and transfer safely.

Most domains don't sell because nobody who needs them ever finds them, or because the seller asks for a number with no basis. Selling well is a process: price, present, distribute, respond, negotiate, close. Here is how to run it.

Step 1: Know your numbers

Before you list, decide three numbers: a buy-now price, a minimum offer and a walk-away floor. Start from a value estimate, check comparable sales, and then use the asking price calculator. Remember what you will actually keep after fees — the fee calculator shows net proceeds.

Step 2: Make it easy to buy

Buyers who type your domain into a browser should see, immediately, that it is for sale, the price, and one clear way to buy. Point the domain at a marketplace lander or build your own with the lander builder. Show a price where you can: listings with a visible buy-now price tend to convert better than "make offer" pages for names under the premium tier.

Step 3: Offer payment flexibility

Many buyers want your name but can't pay in one lump sum. Lease-to-own and payment plans widen your audience dramatically. On BetterNic you can accept up to ten payment methods — from BetterNic-managed escrow to Escrow.com, PayPal for small deals or your own arrangement — so buyers can pay the way they trust.

Step 4: Distribute your listing

List on marketplaces whose buyers match your names. Large registrar networks reach buyers at the moment of search; specialist marketplaces reach brandable shoppers; forums reach investors. Listing on BetterNic is free and costs a 5% commission only when you sell (4% from $50,000 and 3% from $100,000).

Step 5: Do outbound for the right names

Keyword and geographic names often sell faster through outreach: find businesses already using the keyword, companies on weaker extensions, and firms bidding on the term in ads. Personalise each message, include a price range, and respect anti-spam rules such as Canada's CASL. The outreach generator writes a first message and follow-ups.

Step 6: Respond fast and qualify

Reply to inquiries within hours, not days. Ask what the buyer plans to do with the name and their timeline. A buyer with a launch date is worth more of your time than a vague "how much?".

Step 7: Negotiate with structure

Counter with a reason, concede in shrinking steps, and offer lease-to-own before dropping your price below your minimum. Put agreed terms in writing: price, payment method, who pays escrow fees, transfer timeline.

Step 8: Close safely

Use escrow for any sale where you don't already trust the buyer. Never send the authorisation code before funds are secured. Follow the transfer checklist: secure payment, migrate any email, unlock, send the auth code or push, confirm receipt, release funds.

Red flags

  • "Buyers" asking you to pay for an appraisal or certificate before they purchase — a classic scam.
  • Requests to transfer before payment clears.
  • Payment by reversible methods for large amounts.
  • Pressure to skip escrow "to save fees".

After the sale

Record the sale for tax purposes, update your portfolio tracker, and reinvest. Your sale price is also data: it tells you how to price similar names next time.
